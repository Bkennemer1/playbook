Speed matters here: the gap between "you're approved" on the phone and the letter in their inbox should be minutes, not days.</p>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Letter amount: ask the borrower</h2>
  <p>Before issuing, ask the borrower what price range they're shopping. The letter strategy is borrower-driven:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Keep the letter amount within their shopping range</strong> — not automatically at max qualification.</li>
    <li><strong>Tell them they qualify for more</strong> (when true), and that you can update the letter to match any offer in minutes.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>This does two things: the borrower shops with a number that matches their actual intent, and when their offer lands on a seller's desk, the letter doesn't advertise how much more they could pay — which protects their negotiating position.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Who gets it</h2>
  <p>Send the letter to the <strong>borrower and their realtor</strong> (if they have one). The realtor is going to attach it to offers — getting it in their hands directly saves the borrower a forwarding step and puts you in the realtor's inbox as the responsive lender on this deal.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Updated letters: on demand, any time</h2>
  <p>The service standard: <strong>updated letters go out on demand, nights and weekends included.</strong> Offers get written at 8pm on a Saturday, and a letter matched to the offer price is often the difference between the offer going in tonight or Monday. When a borrower or realtor asks for an updated letter, that's a now task, not a tomorrow task.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Letter shelf life: 90 days</h2>
  <p>The pre-approval letter is good for <strong>90 days — tied to the credit report</strong> behind it. When the credit report expires, so does the letter. For long shoppers, that means a fresh hard pull and a re-verification that nothing has changed before a new letter goes out. This is the moment new debt or missed payments surface — see the coaching note in <a href="/phases/application/pull-credit">Pull credit</a>: no new debt while house hunting.</p>
</div>

<div className="callout warn">
  <strong>Update the Arive milestone to Pre-Approved — manually, every time.</strong> The LO sets this by hand when the letter goes out; nothing does it automatically. This isn't bookkeeping trivia: our internal pipeline tools key off the loan's milestone. A pre-approved borrower whose file still shows an earlier milestone is invisible to every downstream system — dashboards, queues, reporting. Letter out = milestone updated. Same action, every time.
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>While they shop: staying in touch</h2>
  <p>Once the letter is out, the borrower enters shopping mode — which can last weeks or months. <strong>Staying in touch is the LO's personal responsibility.</strong> There is currently no automated nurture for pre-approved shoppers; no system checks in on them if you don't. Build your own rhythm — a check-in text every week or two, a call after they tour homes, whatever fits your style. A pre-approved borrower who doesn't hear from you is a pre-approved borrower another lender can steal at offer time.</p>
  <p><em>Known gap: automating pre-approved-shopper nurture is on the company roadmap. Until it exists, manual follow-up is the process.</em></p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Common pitfalls</h2>

  <h3>Letting the letter (and credit) expire mid-shop</h3>
  <p>Track your pre-approved shoppers' 90-day windows. A borrower who finds their house in month four with expired credit needs a re-pull before an updated letter can go out — bad time to discover they financed furniture in month two.</p>

  <h3>Skipping the milestone update</h3>
  <p>Covered in the callout above. Letter out = milestone to Pre-Approved. Every time.</p>

  <h3>Slow letter updates</h3>
  <p>An offer that goes in without a matched letter, or waits overnight for one, is a worse offer. On-demand means on-demand.</p>

  <h3>Going quiet during the shop</h3>
  <p>The pre-approval is the start of the relationship, not the end of your job. Silent LOs lose funded loans to whoever the listing agent recommends at offer time.</p>
</div>
