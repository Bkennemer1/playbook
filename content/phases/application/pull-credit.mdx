  <h2>Overview</h2>
  <p>With the application reviewed and accurate, pull credit. The strategic decision — soft vs. hard, and when to push for hard — is covered in the <a href="/phases/application/lead-intake-qualifying">Lead intake &amp; qualifying conversation</a> step. This page covers the mechanics: the pull itself, the cost handling, and what to review when the report comes back.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>The pull</h2>
  <p>Credit is pulled directly inside Arive. Multiple credit vendors are allowed, but the company standard is <strong>Credco</strong> — most LOs pull through Credco via the Arive integration.</p>
  <p><em>Scribe walkthrough pending — the credit pull screen, including where you select soft vs. hard.</em></p>

  <h3>Soft-to-hard is a fresh pull</h3>
  <p>If a borrower started with a soft pull and is now ready to move forward, there is no conversion or upgrade — you run a fresh hard pull, which generates an entirely new report. Remember the constraint from Step 1: <strong>AUS cannot run on soft pull credit.</strong> The hard pull is the gate to DU/LP and the pre-approval letter.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Who pays for the pull</h2>
  <p>Two options, LO's choice:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>LO pays upfront.</strong> You cover the pull cost yourself at the time of the pull.</li>
    <li><strong>Charge the borrower.</strong> The borrower pays for the initial credit pull directly.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>If you pay upfront, you can be reimbursed at closing — but only under one condition, covered below.</p>
</div>

<div className="callout warn">
  <strong>Reimbursement requires disclosure.</strong> If the LO pays for the credit pull and wants reimbursement at closing, the credit report fee MUST have been disclosed as a line-item fee on the initial loan disclosures that went to the borrower. No disclosure, no reimbursement — you eat the cost. When in doubt, make sure the credit report fee is on the fee sheet before disclosures go out.
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>When the pull fails: frozen credit</h2>
  <p>Occasionally a pull fails because the borrower has a security freeze on one or more bureaus. It's manageable and usually resolves same-day:</p>
  <ol>
    <li>Tell the borrower which bureau(s) are frozen — the failed pull will indicate it.</li>
    <li>The borrower unfreezes online with each frozen bureau (Experian, TransUnion, Equifax — each has its own site/app and takes a few minutes).</li>
    <li>Once they confirm the freeze is lifted, re-pull. Usually works the same day.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>Worth asking during the qualifying call if the borrower has ever frozen their credit — many did during a data-breach scare and forgot. Catching it early saves the failed-pull round trip.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Reviewing the report — before AUS</h2>
  <p>Do a full tradeline review before running AUS. You're checking the report against the story the borrower told you and confirming the DTI inputs are real:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Scores</strong> — in the range the borrower estimated? If dramatically lower, pause and diagnose before AUS.</li>
    <li><strong>Tradelines and debts</strong> — every monthly obligation that will hit DTI: car loans, student loans, credit cards, other mortgages. Do the payments match what the borrower described?</li>
    <li><strong>Payment history</strong> — late pays the borrower didn't mention? Recent delinquencies?</li>
    <li><strong>Surprises</strong> — collections, charge-offs, judgments, or accounts the borrower didn't disclose. Anything unexpected gets a conversation with the borrower before you run AUS, not after.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>The goal: when you run DU/LP in the next step, nothing in the findings should surprise you. If the report changed the picture — score lower than expected, debt load heavier, derogatory history — you may need to revisit program selection or pricing before AUS.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Report shelf life: 90 days</h2>
  <p>A credit report is good for <strong>90 days</strong>. After that, it must be re-pulled. This matters most for pre-approved shoppers who take months to find a house — if the file drags past 90 days, budget for a fresh pull, and know that scores and tradelines can shift. A borrower who barely qualified on the first pull can fall out of qualification on the re-pull if they've taken on new debt (new truck, new credit card) while shopping. Coach pre-approved borrowers explicitly: no new debt while house hunting.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Rapid rescore</h2>
  <p>When a score improvement would change pricing or qualification — the borrower is a few points shy of the next pricing tier, or just below a program minimum — rapid rescore is part of our toolkit. Typical scenario: borrower pays down a credit card balance, and the rescore pushes the updated balance through the bureaus in days instead of waiting a full reporting cycle.</p>
  <p>Rescores run through Credco. If you haven't run one before, loop in Bryce or Dean on strategy first — knowing <em>which</em> action will move the score (and by roughly how much) is the skill, and burning a rescore on the wrong tradeline wastes time and money.</p>
</div>
