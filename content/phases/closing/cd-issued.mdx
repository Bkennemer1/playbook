  <h2>Overview</h2> 

  <p>Every gate item is satisfied — the Closing Disclosure can now be issued. Once it reaches the borrower, TRID's three-business-day waiting period begins, and closing cannot happen until it expires.</p> 

  <p><strong>How the CD gets issued depends on the lender</strong>, and the difference matters for how much Emily has to do.</p> 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>Two paths</h2> 

  

  <h3>UWM — automatic</h3> 

  <p>Once every item on the gate checklist is satisfied, <strong>UWM automatically generates the initial CD and sends it to the borrower</strong>, which starts the three-day clock. Emily doesn't request anything. Her job is to clear the gate and then confirm the CD actually went out.</p> 

  

  <h3>Everyone else — request required</h3> 

  <p>Kind Lending, PennyMac, Loan Store, and most other lenders require Emily to <strong>submit a request-for-CD form in their portal.</strong> The form is the trigger. Important: those portals typically won't let the form be submitted until the same gate items are satisfied — so the checklist still governs, it just doesn't fire automatically at the end.</p> 

</div> 

  

<div className="callout warn"> 

  <strong>Know which path your lender is on.</strong> The failure mode is assuming automatic when it isn't: the gate clears, Emily moves on, and nobody notices for two days that no CD was ever requested — two days burned off a closing timeline that had no slack. On any non-UWM file, requesting the CD is an explicit task, not something that happens on its own. 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>The three-day clock</h2> 

  <p>TRID requires the borrower to receive the CD at least <strong>three business days before consummation</strong>. The lender systems enforce this — you won't be able to schedule a closing that violates it — but understanding it is what lets you plan backward from the closing date instead of discovering a problem.</p> 

  <p>Practical implication: <strong>the CD issue date determines whether the closing date holds.</strong> Work backward from the scheduled closing, count back three business days, and that's your deadline for getting the CD out. Miss it and the closing moves — there's no waiver for convenience.</p> 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>After the CD goes out</h2> 

  <ul> 

    <li><strong>Confirm the borrower received it.</strong> A CD sitting unopened in a spam folder is a CD that will cause problems at signing.</li> 

    <li><strong>Watch for changes that trigger a re-disclosure.</strong> Certain changes after the CD is issued require a corrected CD and can restart the waiting period. If numbers move materially, flag it before it becomes a closing-day surprise.</li> 

    <li><strong>Move on to closing coordination</strong> — title, figures, and borrower preparation run during this window.</li> 

  </ul> 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>Common pitfalls</h2> 

  

  <h3>Assuming the CD went out</h3> 

  <p>On non-UWM lenders it doesn't go out until requested. Verify, don't assume.</p> 

  

  <h3>Not planning backward from the closing date</h3> 

  <p>The three-day rule isn't flexible. A CD issued too late moves the closing, which moves the seller's plans, the movers, the rate lock expiration, and everything else.</p> 

  

  <h3>Late changes that restart the clock</h3> 

  <p>Material changes after CD issuance can require re-disclosure. The later a change lands, the more expensive it is — catch fee and figure problems during the balancing work, not after.</p> 

</div>
