<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>Overview</h2> 

  <p>The loan is signed and funded. What happens next is, honestly, the least developed part of our process — and the highest-leverage place to improve it. This is the moment a borrower is happiest with us. It's where reviews, referrals, and repeat business come from, and right now we mostly leave it to chance.</p> 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>What happens automatically</h2> 

  <p>When the Arive milestone changes to <strong>Docs Signed</strong>, a review request email fires to the borrower automatically. It comes from the LO on the file.</p> 

  <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Loan Closed - Congratulations!</p> 

  <div className="email-template">{`[LO Name] & The Guarantee Mortgage Team would love your feedback 

  

We're grateful you chose us for the purchase of your new home. Referrals and online reviews from clients like you really help my business — that's why I hope you'll take just a moment and leave us a review on Google! 

  

[Leave us a review on Google — 5 star graphic/link] 

  

It's a very quick and easy way for you to make a difference in how our company provides services in the future and give recognition to everyone who played a role in getting you to closing! 

  

Enjoy your new chapter! 

  

Thank you, 

  

[Guarantee Mortgage Company]`}</div> 

</div> 

  

<div className="callout warn"> 

  <strong>Two known problems with this email — be aware of both.</strong> 

  <br /><br /> 

  <strong>1. It fires at Docs Signed, not at funding.</strong> The subject line says "Loan Closed," but at Docs Signed the loan hasn't funded yet. If anything goes wrong between signing and funding, we've already congratulated the borrower and asked for a review on a loan that hasn't closed. 

  <br /><br /> 

  <strong>2. The timing is too early regardless.</strong> Closing day is chaotic for a borrower — moving, utilities, keys, family. A review request landing in the middle of it competes with everything else and gets ignored. Waiting a day or two, once they're in the house and the dust has settled, would almost certainly convert better. 

  <br /><br /> 

  Both are on the list to fix. Until then, know that the automated email may reach the borrower at an awkward moment — and a personal follow-up from you carries more weight anyway. 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>What happens after that — nothing consistent</h2> 

  <p>Beyond the automated email, post-close follow-up is entirely up to the individual LO. Some reach out personally, some don't. There's no defined cadence, no reminder, no system.</p> 

  <p><strong>That's a gap, and it's worth closing on your own files even before the company solves it.</strong> A funded borrower is the warmest lead you will ever have. They just had a major life event go well, partly because of you, and they know people in the same situation.</p> 

</div> 

  

<div className="step-page-section"> 

  <h2>What good looks like, until there's a system</h2> 

  <p>No company-mandated process exists yet. If you want a default that works:</p> 

  <ul> 

    <li><strong>A personal message a day or two after closing</strong> — not the automated one. A short text or call once they're in the house. This is where the review ask actually lands.</li> 

    <li><strong>Thank the referral source.</strong> If a realtor or past client sent them, close the loop. That's how referral relationships compound.</li> 

    <li><strong>Note the anniversary.</strong> A message at the one-year mark costs nothing and puts you back in mind before a refi or a move.</li> 

  </ul> 

  <p>None of this is enforced. All of it works.</p> 

</div> 

  

<div className="callout info"> 

  <strong>The milestone problem shows up here too.</strong> The review email fires off a milestone change. If the milestone never gets set — the recurring problem covered in <a href="/phases/closing/signing-through-funded">Signing through funded</a> — the borrower receives nothing at all. No congratulations, no review request. From every system's perspective, their loan is still in progress. Another reason the closing milestones matter. 

</div>
