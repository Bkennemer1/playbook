<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Overview</h2>
  <p>The file comes back from underwriting <strong>Approved with Conditions</strong> — the loan is approved, subject to a list of items that must be satisfied before Clear to Close. This is the normal outcome; a first-pass approval with zero conditions is rare.</p>
  <p>Everything in this phase runs off one decision made at the top: <strong>Danielle reads the approval letter and splits the conditions</strong> between herself and Hunter. Get that split right and the file moves. Get it wrong — or skip it — and conditions sit unowned while the closing date approaches.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Where conditions live</h2>
  <p>Two places, and the distinction matters:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>The lender portal is the source of truth.</strong> Underwriting posts conditions there. That's the authoritative list, and it's what has to be satisfied to get CTC.</li>
    <li><strong>Arive is the working copy.</strong> Danielle manually types each condition into the Arive Conditions tab. That's where the team tracks status, attaches documents, and assigns ownership.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Manual mirroring means the two can drift. If a condition exists in the portal but was never entered in Arive, nobody's working it — and it won't surface until resubmission gets rejected.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Danielle's triage</h2>

  <h3>1. Read the approval letter in full</h3>
  <p>Every condition, start to finish, before assigning anything. Conditions frequently reference each other, and reading them as a set tells you which ones are actually one item split across two lines.</p>

  <h3>2. Enter each condition into Arive</h3>
  <p>Manually, in the Conditions tab. This is data entry with consequences — a condition typed vaguely is a condition someone misinterprets three days later.</p>

  <h3>3. Categorize</h3>
  <p>Each condition falls into one of five buckets, and the bucket determines who owns it:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Borrower documents</strong> → Hunter</li>
    <li><strong>Title</strong> → Danielle</li>
    <li><strong>Appraisal</strong> → Danielle</li>
    <li><strong>Income</strong> → usually Hunter for collection; route to the LO if it needs an income decision</li>
    <li><strong>LO decision</strong> → the LO on the file (structure, program, or qualification calls)</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>4. Assign in Arive</h3>
  <p>Use Arive's condition assignment — assign each condition to the team member who owns it. <strong>Arive automatically emails the assignee</strong> telling them which conditions on which file are theirs, with the list. That email is the handoff.</p>

  <h3>5. Flip the milestone</h3>
  <p>Danielle manually updates the Arive milestone to <strong>Approved w/ Conditions</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div className="callout warn">
  <strong>Assign in Arive — don't hand off verbally.</strong> Today Hunter and Danielle often sit in the same room, so conditions get divided by talking across a desk. That works right up until it doesn't: someone's out, someone's remote, someone misremembers, or the team grows. Arive's assignment feature creates a record and sends the assignee a written list. Use it every time, even when the person is five feet away.
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>The condition status ladder</h2>
  <p>Every condition in Arive moves through a status progression. Knowing where each one sits is how you know whether the file is ready to go back to underwriting:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Requested</strong> — the condition is entered and the document has been asked for</li>
    <li><strong>Re-requested</strong> — what came back didn't satisfy it; asked again</li>
    <li><strong>Collected</strong> — the document is in hand, pending review</li>
    <li><strong>Ready to send</strong> — reviewed, satisfies the condition, cleared to go back to UW</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Every condition must reach "Ready to send" before anything is resubmitted.</strong> That's the gate — see the resubmission step.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>What happens next</h2>
  <p>Hunter and Danielle work their assigned conditions in parallel — <a href="/phases/uw-decision/borrower-conditions">Hunter on everything borrower-facing</a>, <a href="/phases/uw-decision/docs-and-arive-conditions">Danielle on everything back-office</a> — until all conditions are marked ready to send. Then Danielle packages and resubmits.</p>
  <p>Expect roughly <strong>one additional round</strong> on a typical file: resubmit, underwriting reviews, a few new or re-requested conditions come back, clear those, resubmit again, then Clear to Close.</p>
</div>
