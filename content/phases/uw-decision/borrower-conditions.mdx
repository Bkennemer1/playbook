<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Overview</h2>
  <p>Danielle has assigned Hunter his conditions in Arive — he receives the automated email listing which conditions on which file are his. These are the borrower-facing items: documents only the borrower can produce, letters of explanation, updated statements, gift documentation, anything requiring the borrower to act.</p>
  <p><strong>Timing: borrower-facing conditions reach the borrower within 24 hours of AWC.</strong> This is the tightest SLA in the playbook, and for good reason — every day a condition sits unsent is a day added to the closing timeline, on a contract clock that doesn't pause.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>The sequence — same pattern as Pre-W</h2>
  <p>Hunter already established the relationship during <a href="/phases/handoff/pre-underwriting-review">Pre-Underwriting Review</a>. This runs the same way:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Review the assigned conditions first.</strong> Understand what each one is actually asking for before contacting the borrower. A condition you don't understand becomes a document request the borrower can't satisfy.</li>
    <li><strong>Call the borrower — every time.</strong> Approval-with-conditions is a moment borrowers misread. Some hear "approved" and relax; others hear "conditions" and panic that something's wrong. The call sets the frame: <em>you're approved, here's what's left, here's the timeline.</em> Then tell them the list is coming.</li>
    <li><strong>Send one consolidated request.</strong> Through Arive Client Needs — everything at once, not a drip of individual asks. Conditions frequently associate directly with Client Needs items in Arive; use that link so uploads land against the right condition.</li>
    <li><strong>Set the follow-up date</strong> and chase until every item clears.</li>
  </ol>
</div>

<div className="callout">
  <strong>Frame it as progress, not a problem.</strong> "Approved with conditions" sounds worse to a borrower than it is. The call is where you tell them this is the normal, expected next step — the underwriter approved the loan and needs a few final items. A borrower who understands that works the list quickly. A borrower who thinks something went wrong gets anxious, calls their realtor, and slows everything down.
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Working the list</h2>
  <p>As documents come in, move each condition up the status ladder in Arive:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Collected</strong> once the document arrives</li>
    <li><strong>Ready to send</strong> once you've reviewed it and confirmed it actually satisfies the condition</li>
  </ul>
  <p>That review step is the one that matters. A document that arrives isn't a condition cleared — it's a condition <em>maybe</em> cleared. Read it against what the condition asked for. If it doesn't satisfy, mark it <strong>re-requested</strong> and go back to the borrower with specifically what's still missing. Catching an insufficient document here costs one phone call. Catching it after resubmission costs a full underwriting cycle.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Common condition types Hunter owns</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Letters of explanation</strong> — address discrepancies, credit inquiries, employment gaps, large deposits. Must be signed and dated.</li>
    <li><strong>Updated statements</strong> — bank statements that aged out during processing, or additional months requested</li>
    <li><strong>Proof of liquidation</strong> — evidence that funds moved from an investment or retirement account</li>
    <li><strong>Earnest money documentation</strong> — receipt from title, evidence the EMD cleared the borrower's account</li>
    <li><strong>Gift documentation</strong> — gift letter plus the donor and transfer trail</li>
    <li><strong>Business documentation</strong> for self-employed borrowers — YTD P&amp;L, business bank statements</li>
    <li><strong>Updated paystubs</strong> when the originals aged past the recency window</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>When a condition isn't Hunter's to solve</h2>
  <p>Some conditions assigned to Hunter turn out to need an LO decision — an income question, a structure change, something about qualification. Route it to the LO.</p>
  <p><strong>Routing it doesn't transfer ownership.</strong> Same rule as Pre-W: Hunter remains the follow-up owner until the item is resolved. Nothing gets orphaned because it's "with the LO."</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Common pitfalls</h2>

  <h3>Sending the list without calling first</h3>
  <p>A cold document request against an approval the borrower doesn't understand gets slow, anxious responses. Two minutes on the phone changes the whole dynamic.</p>

  <h3>Marking a condition ready to send without actually reading the document</h3>
  <p>The most expensive mistake in this phase. An insufficient document that reaches underwriting comes back as a re-request and adds a full cycle.</p>

  <h3>Drip-feeding requests</h3>
  <p>Three separate asks over three days trains the borrower to expect endless requests. One complete list, worked to completion.</p>

  <h3>Losing the 24-hour window</h3>
  <p>Conditions that sit two or three days before reaching the borrower push the whole timeline. This is the fastest-moving SLA in the process for a reason.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Who to ask if you're stuck</h2>
  <p>A condition you can't interpret: Danielle first (she read the full approval letter), then Bryce.<br />Income, credit, or qualification questions: the LO on the file.<br />A borrower who's stalled or unresponsive: loop in the LO — the relationship may need their weight.</p>
</div>
