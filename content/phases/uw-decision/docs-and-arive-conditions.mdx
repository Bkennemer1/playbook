  <p>Danielle owns two things in this phase: the back-office conditions assigned to her, and — once everything is cleared — <strong>packaging the whole file back to underwriting.</strong> The resubmission is hers regardless of who cleared what.</p>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Danielle's conditions</h2>
  <p>Everything that doesn't require the borrower:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Title</strong> — commitment issues, payoff figures, vesting corrections, exceptions that need clearing, seller-side documentation</li>
    <li><strong>Appraisal</strong> — value conditions, repair requirements, re-inspections, appraisal corrections</li>
    <li><strong>HOI</strong> — binder or policy corrections, coverage amount adjustments, mortgagee clause fixes</li>
    <li><strong>Invoices and fees</strong> — final invoices underwriting needs for the file</li>
    <li><strong>Lender portal items</strong> — anything satisfied by data or documents on our side rather than the borrower's</li>
  </ul>
  <p>She works these against the same status ladder — <strong>collected</strong> when the item arrives, <strong>ready to send</strong> once she's confirmed it satisfies the condition.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Resubmission: the batch rule</h2>
  <p>This is the most important mechanic in the phase, and it's counterintuitive if you're used to sending things as they arrive.</p>
  <p><strong>Nothing goes back to underwriting until every condition in Arive is marked "Ready to send."</strong> Not the borrower's items as they trickle in, not title the day it clears. Everything, together, once.</p>
  <p>Then:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Confirm every condition is at Ready to send</strong> — that's the gate</li>
    <li><strong>Download all documentation attached to each condition</strong> from Arive</li>
    <li><strong>Upload the complete package back to the underwriter</strong> through the lender portal</li>
  </ol>
  <p>Why batch: <strong>most lenders prefer it.</strong> Piecemeal documentation means the underwriter picks the file up, reviews one item, sets it down, and picks it up again days later — each touch a new place in their queue. One complete package gets one clean review. We collect everything from every source — borrower, title, appraisal, insurance — in Arive, then submit it all at once.</p>
</div>

<div className="callout warn">
  <strong>The gate is every condition, not just yours.</strong> Danielle can't resubmit while Hunter still has an outstanding borrower item, and vice versa. If the file is stalled on one side, that's a conversation — not a partial submission. Check the Conditions tab, confirm the whole list is at Ready to send, then package.
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Expect another round</h2>
  <p>A typical file cycles <strong>about one extra time</strong>: resubmit → underwriting reviews → a few new or re-requested conditions come back → clear those → resubmit → Clear to Close.</p>
  <p>When a second round comes back, it's the same process from the top: Danielle reads the new conditions, enters them in Arive, categorizes, assigns, and the split repeats. New conditions on a second round are often re-requests — something submitted the first time didn't satisfy the underwriter. Read those carefully; the underwriter is telling you specifically what was wrong.</p>
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Common pitfalls</h2>

  <h3>Resubmitting before everything is ready</h3>
  <p>A partial package invites a partial review and usually a fresh round of conditions. The gate exists for a reason.</p>

  <h3>Conditions in the portal that never made it into Arive</h3>
  <p>Because the mirroring is manual, a missed condition is invisible to the team — it just doesn't exist in the working copy. Before resubmitting, cross-check the Arive list against the lender portal's list. They should match.</p>

  <h3>Marking ready to send without reviewing</h3>
  <p>Same failure mode as Hunter's side. A document that arrived isn't a condition satisfied until someone confirms it actually answers what was asked.</p>

  <h3>Silent stalls</h3>
  <p>A condition waiting on a third party — title, the appraiser, an insurance agent — can sit for days without anyone noticing, because the status doesn't change. Chase third parties actively; they don't know your closing date.</p>
</div>
