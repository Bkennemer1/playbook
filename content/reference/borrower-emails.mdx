<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>What this page is</h2>
  <p>Arive fires automated emails to borrowers when a loan's milestone changes. These are <strong>automatic</strong> — moving a file's status in Arive sends the email. Nobody hits send.</p>
  <p>This page catalogs every milestone-triggered borrower email: what fires it, who receives it, and the exact content the borrower sees. Read the email for a milestone before you move a file into it — the borrower is about to receive it, so the file should be right first.</p>
</div>

<div className="callout warn">
  <strong>Milestone changes are borrower-facing events.</strong> Flipping a status in Arive isn't internal bookkeeping — it sends mail. Don't advance a milestone to tidy up your pipeline view, and don't advance one before the thing it announces is actually true.
</div>

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>Recipients</h2>
  <p>Milestone emails go to the <strong>borrower, co-borrower, and the realtor</strong> automatically when they're on the file. You don't pick recipients — Arive pulls them from the loan.</p>
  <p>Practical implication: if the realtor's contact info is wrong or missing on the file, they silently don't get these updates. If a realtor tells you they're out of the loop, check the file's contacts before assuming the automation is broken.</p>
</div>

<hr style={{border: 'none', borderTop: '1px solid var(--border)', margin: '32px 0'}} />

<div className="step-page-section">
  <h2>LOAN SETUP</h2>
  <p><strong>Fires when:</strong> the LO manually moves the file to <strong>Loan Setup</strong> in Arive — typically right after the borrower goes under contract.</p>
  <p><strong>Subject line:</strong> Your Loan Is Being Set Up – Next Steps Inside</p>
  <p><strong>Goes to:</strong> borrower, co-borrower, realtor</p>

  <h3>What the borrower sees</h3>
  <div className="email-template">{`NEXT STEP: REVIEW & SIGN YOUR DISCLOSURES
Estimated Closing: [close date]

[Borrower names],

We're officially setting up your loan file and moving into the next phase of the process. If this is your first home purchase, don't worry — we'll guide you through each step.

WHAT'S HAPPENING NOW
We're preparing your file for underwriting review. Within the next 24–48 hours, you'll receive an email from EaseDocs with your initial loan disclosures to review and eSign.

These disclosures are a required first step and allow us to move forward with underwriting, ordering the appraisal, and coordinating title and insurance.

IMPORTANT NOTES
- The numbers you'll see are estimates, not final figures. It's normal for them to change as we complete the appraisal, title work, and final underwriting.
- Even though these are preliminary, we do need you to review and eSign them so we can submit your loan to underwriting and keep everything moving forward.
- Staying on track with each step helps protect your closing date, and this is step one.

We're working to meet your [close date] closing. The sooner these are signed, the more breathing room we have if anything needs adjustment later — which helps avoid last-minute stress as we get closer to closing.

If you have questions about anything in the disclosures, reach out before you sign — that's what we're here for. Otherwise, we'll keep things moving on our end and let you know as soon as the next step is complete.

Bryce Kennemer • Guarantee Mortgage • NMLS #1808043
guaranteemc.com`}</div>

  <h3>What this commits you to</h3>
  <p>This email makes two promises on your behalf the moment it sends:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Disclosures within 24–48 hours.</strong> The borrower now expects an e-sign request in two days. Don't move a file to Loan Setup unless you're ready to get disclosures out on that timeline.</li>
    <li><strong>An email from a specific e-sign system.</strong> See the defect note below.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div className="callout warn">
  <strong>Known defect in this template: "EaseDocs" is hardcoded.</strong> EaseDocs is UWM's e-signing system. Every lender uses a different one. So a borrower whose loan is with any other lender is told to watch for an email from EaseDocs — which will never arrive, while the real disclosure email sits unopened from a company name they were never told about. Until the template is fixed to be lender-aware, <strong>tell the borrower directly which e-sign system to expect for their specific lender</strong> when you move the file to Loan Setup.
</div>
